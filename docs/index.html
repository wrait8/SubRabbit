Packet sniffer & frequency analyzer

Point it at a channel and watch RF packets come in with live RSSI and link-quality readout, or sweep a band to find which channels actually have traffic on them before you commit to listening on one. Both run straight from the serial console — no companion app needed.

firmware / capture

Record & replay

Two capture paths, depending on what you're working with. recraw / playraw grab the raw RF samples bit for bit — useful while you're still figuring out what a signal is. Once you know a transmitter sends a simple fixed code (most remotes, many sensors), recsig / playsig capture and reproduce just that code.

raw
recraw / playraw — full-fidelity sample capture
fixed-code
recsig / playsig — captured code, replayed on demand
use on
your own remotes, sensors and test gear

Jamming & brute-force

jam transmits continuously on a channel; brute steps through timing/code space against a receiver. Both are active, transmitting modes — unlike everything above, they put RF energy on air deliberately and can affect other people's equipment. Read the box below before you use either one.

jam

brute

get it running

Flash the firmware

Three ways to get SubRabbit onto the board, from quickest to most hands-on.

Web flasher — no install

Open wrait8.github.io/WFlasher in Chrome or Edge, plug the board in over USB, pick the firmware and flash straight from the page via Web Serial.

CLI tool

Run Serial.py from the repo's /Serial folder for an interactive or scripted flash from the command line.

Source + Arduino IDE

Clone the repo, open SubRabbit.ino in the Arduino IDE, and upload it yourself — the route to take if you're modifying the firmware.

read before you transmit

Most of this firmware receives only — sniffing and capture don't put anything on air. jam and brute are different: they transmit, and radio transmission is regulated by law almost everywhere, not just by etiquette.

Jamming — deliberately interfering with radio spectrum — is illegal in most countries, full stop, including on license-free ISM bands, regardless of your intent. There isn't a "responsible use" version of continuous jamming against anything other than a shielded test setup you own.

Brute-forcing a code against a receiver you don't own — a gate, a garage door, a car, an alarm — is unauthorized access in most jurisdictions, separate from any radio-law violation. Use jam and brute only inside a shielded enclosure or against your own equipment that you're explicitly allowed to test.

Everything else — sniffing, capture, replay — still only ever acts on your own devices and remotes, or equipment you're otherwise authorized to test. Check your country's radio regulations (FCC Part 15 in the US, ETSI in the EU, or your local equivalent) before you transmit anything.
